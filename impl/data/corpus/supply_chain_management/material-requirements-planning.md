# Material requirements planning

Production planning, scheduling, and inventory control system

**Material requirements planning** (**MRP**) is a [production planning](/wiki/Production_planning "Production planning"), [scheduling](/wiki/Scheduling "Scheduling"), and [inventory](/wiki/Inventory "Inventory") control system used to [manage](/wiki/Management "Management") [manufacturing](/wiki/Manufacturing "Manufacturing") processes. Most MRP systems are [software](/wiki/Computer_software "Computer software")-based, but it is possible to conduct MRP by hand as well.

An MRP system is intended to simultaneously meet three objectives:

- Ensure raw materials are available for [production](/wiki/Manufacturing "Manufacturing") and [products](/wiki/Product_(business) "Product (business)") are available for [delivery](/wiki/Delivery_(commerce) "Delivery (commerce)") to customers.
- Maintain the lowest possible material and product levels in store
- Plan manufacturing activities, delivery schedules and [purchasing](/wiki/Purchasing "Purchasing") activities.

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Material_requirements_planning&veaction=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History") | [edit source](/w/index.php?title=Material_requirements_planning&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section's source code: History")]

Prior to MRP, and before computers dominated industry, [reorder point (ROP)](/wiki/Reorder_point "Reorder point")/reorder-quantity (ROQ) type methods like [EOQ (economic order quantity)](/wiki/Economic_order_quantity "Economic order quantity") had been used in manufacturing and inventory management.[[1]](#cite_note-1) MRP was computerized by the aero engine makers Rolls-Royce and General Electric in the early 1950s but not commercialized by them. It was then 'reinvented' to supply the [Polaris program](/wiki/Polaris_(UK_nuclear_programme) "Polaris (UK nuclear programme)") and then, in 1964, as a response to the [Toyota Manufacturing Program](/wiki/Toyota_Production_System "Toyota Production System"), [Joseph Orlicky](/wiki/Joseph_Orlicky "Joseph Orlicky") developed material requirements planning (MRP).

Early MRP users in the 1960s and 1970s included [Black & Decker](/wiki/Black_%26_Decker "Black & Decker"), [Twin Disc](/wiki/Twin_Disc "Twin Disc"), Markem, [Steelcase](/wiki/Steelcase "Steelcase"), and American Sterilizer.[[2]](#cite_note-gray19860127-2) The first company was Black and Decker in 1964, with Dick Alban as project leader. Orlicky's 1975 book *Material Requirements Planning* has the subtitle *The New Way of Life in Production and Inventory Management*.[[3]](#cite_note-3) By 1975, MRP was implemented in 700 companies. This number had grown to about 8,000 by 1981.
