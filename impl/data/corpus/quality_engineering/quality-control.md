**Quality control** (**QC**) is a process by which entities review the quality of all factors involved in [production](/wiki/Production_(economics) "Production (economics)"). [ISO 9000](/wiki/ISO_9000 "ISO 9000") defines quality control as "a part of [quality management](/wiki/Quality_management "Quality management") focused on fulfilling quality requirements".[[1]](#cite_note-1)

This approach places emphasis on three aspects (enshrined in standards such as ISO 9001):[[2]](#cite_note-PraxiomISO17-2)[[3]](#cite_note-AftFunda97-3)

1. Elements such as controls, job management, defined and well managed processes,[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5) performance and integrity criteria, and identification of records
2. Competence, such as knowledge, skills, experience, and qualifications
3. Soft elements, such as personnel, [integrity](/wiki/Integrity "Integrity"), [confidence](/wiki/Confidence "Confidence"), [organizational culture](/wiki/Organizational_culture "Organizational culture"), [motivation](/wiki/Motivation "Motivation"), [team spirit](/wiki/Team_spirit "Team spirit"), and quality relationships.

[Inspection](/wiki/Inspection "Inspection") is a major component of quality control, where physical product is examined visually (or the end results of a service are analyzed). Product inspectors will be provided with lists and descriptions of unacceptable [product defects](/wiki/Product_defect "Product defect") such as [cracks](/wiki/Fracture "Fracture") or surface [blemishes](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/blemish "wikt:blemish") for example.[[3]](#cite_note-AftFunda97-3)

## History and introduction

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&veaction=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History and introduction") | [edit source](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section's source code: History and introduction")]

See also: [History of technology](/wiki/History_of_technology "History of technology")

Early [stone tools](/wiki/Stone_tools "Stone tools") such as [anvils](/wiki/Anvil "Anvil") had no holes and were not designed as [interchangeable parts](/wiki/Interchangeable_parts "Interchangeable parts"). [Mass production](/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production") established processes for the creation of parts and system with identical dimensions and design, but these processes are not uniform and hence some customers were unsatisfied with the result. Quality control separates the act of [testing](/wiki/Test_method "Test method") [products](/wiki/Product_(business) "Product (business)") to uncover defects from the decision to allow or deny product release, which may be determined by fiscal constraints.[[6]](#cite_note-Shewhart-6) For contract work, particularly work awarded by government agencies, quality control issues are among the top reasons for not renewing a contract.[[7]](#cite_note-7)

The simplest form of quality control was a sketch of the desired item. If the item did not match the sketch, the item was rejected, in a simple [Go/no go](/wiki/Go/no_go "Go/no go") procedure. However, manufacturers soon found it was difficult and costly to make parts be exactly like their depiction; hence around 1840 tolerance limits were introduced, wherein a design would function if its parts were measured to be within the limits. Quality was thus precisely defined using devices such as [plug gauges](/wiki/Plug_gauge "Plug gauge") and [ring gauges](/wiki/Ring_gauge "Ring gauge"). However, this did not address the problem of defective items; recycling or disposing of the [waste](/wiki/Waste "Waste") adds to the cost of production, as does trying to reduce the defect rate. Various methods have been proposed to prioritize quality control issues and determine whether to leave them unaddressed or use [quality assurance](/wiki/Quality_assurance "Quality assurance") techniques to improve and stabilize production.[[6]](#cite_note-Shewhart-6)

## Notable approaches

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&veaction=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Notable approaches") | [edit source](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section's source code: Notable approaches")]

There is a tendency for individual consultants and organizations to name their own unique approaches to quality control—a few of these have ended up in widespread use:

| Terminology | Approximate year of first use | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statistical quality control (SQC) | 1930s | The application of statistical methods (specifically [control charts](/wiki/Control_chart "Control chart") and [acceptance sampling](/wiki/Acceptance_sampling "Acceptance sampling")) to quality control[[8]](#cite_note-Juran-8): 556 |
| Total quality control (TQC) | 1956 | Popularized by [Armand V. Feigenbaum](/wiki/Armand_V._Feigenbaum "Armand V. Feigenbaum") in a [Harvard Business Review](/wiki/Harvard_Business_Review "Harvard Business Review") article[[9]](#cite_note-9) and book of the same name;[[10]](#cite_note-10) stresses involvement of departments in addition to production (e.g., accounting, design, finance, human resources, marketing, purchasing, sales) |
| [Statistical process control](/wiki/Statistical_process_control "Statistical process control") (SPC) | 1960s | The use of [control charts](/wiki/Control_chart "Control chart") to monitor an individual industrial process and feed back performance to the operators responsible for that process; inspired by [control systems](/wiki/Control_system "Control system") |
| Company-wide quality control (CWQC) | 1968 | Japanese-style total quality control.[[11]](#cite_note-Ishikawa-11) |
| [Total quality management](/wiki/Total_quality_management "Total quality management") (TQM) | 1985 | Quality movement originating in the [United States Department of Defense](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") that uses (in part) the techniques of statistical quality control to drive continuous organizational improvement[[12]](#cite_note-12) |
| [Six Sigma](/wiki/Six_Sigma "Six Sigma") (6σ) | 1986 | Statistical quality control applied to business strategy;[[13]](#cite_note-13) originated by [Motorola](/wiki/Motorola "Motorola") |
| [Lean Six Sigma](/wiki/Lean_Six_Sigma "Lean Six Sigma") (L6σ) | 2001 | Six Sigma applied with the principles of [lean manufacturing](/wiki/Lean_manufacturing "Lean manufacturing") and/or [lean enterprise](/wiki/Lean_enterprise "Lean enterprise"); originated by Wheat *et al.*[[14]](#cite_note-WheatLeaning01-14) |

## In project management

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&veaction=edit&section=3 "Edit section: In project management") | [edit source](/w/index.php?title=Quality_control&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section's source code: In project management")]

In [project management](/wiki/Project_management "Project management"), quality control requires the project manager and/or the project team to inspect the accomplished work to ensure its alignment with the project scope.[[15]](#cite_note-15) In practice, projects typically have a dedicated quality control team which focuses on this area.[[16]](#cite_note-RoseProject14-16)
